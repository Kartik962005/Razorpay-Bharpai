contribution to the batch (₹39,518). A ₹39,522 overdue invoice.

Both policies saw the identical case, the identical customer and the identical random
draws. The only difference is how the customer's reply was read.

## What happened

The customer replied *"paisa Friday ko bhej dunga"* — I will send the money on Friday.
Both policies classified it correctly as a promise to pay. Only the model resolved the date.

The reply arrived on Thursday 6 August. The pattern-matching policy resolved "Friday" to the
next day, 7 August, so its promise-grace rule (R41) lapsed on the 8th and it was free to nudge
again on the 9th. It did, and the customer replied
*"bahut messages aa rahe hain, band karo"* — too many messages, stop. The case closed as an
opt-out and ₹39,522 was lost.

The model resolved a later date, so R41 held the case silent until 12 August. The customer paid
on the 11th, unprompted, exactly as they had said they would.

Neither reading is obviously "wrong" about the word Friday — the point is that the cost of being
a few days early was the entire ₹39,522, and the customer had already told us when to expect it.

This is the entire measured value of the language model on a 500-case batch: one case, and
an explainable one. The report prints the comparison in both directions — the deterministic
planner beat the model-advised one on zero cases.
